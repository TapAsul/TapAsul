Cost & selling-price calculator

Fill this in with your real numbers and the calculator works out what to charge. Everything stays in this browser — nothing is sent anywhere. Read PRICING-GUIDE.md in the site folder if you want the step-by-step explanation.

1 · The batch you are pricing
2 · Raw materials — per unit
3 · Labour & one-off costs

One-off setup = artwork prep, test prints, dies, or a supplier minimum order. It is spread across every unit in the batch.

4 · Transport

If you charge the customer more than the courier costs you, the difference is margin. If you charge less, you are absorbing the difference — that is normal for a launch.

5 · Monthly operating expenses

These are the bills you pay even when you sell nothing. They are spread across the units you expect to sell each month.

6 · Your targets
How this works — read me first
  1. Cost per unit = materials + (your hourly rate × minutes per unit ÷ 60) + (one-off setup ÷ units) + (courier cost ÷ units per parcel).
  2. Overheads per unit = your monthly bills ÷ units you expect to sell each month. These are real costs even if you sell one card.
  3. Break-even price = cost per unit + overheads per unit. Below this you lose money on every sale.
  4. Target price = break-even ÷ (1 − discount) ÷ (1 − margin). This is the smallest price that still hits your margin after a discount.
  5. Recommended shelf price rounds the target up to a number that looks like a price people want to pay, then shows what you actually earn.
  6. Hosting is recurring. A customer who stays 12 months at ₱149/month is worth ₱1,788 — that is what makes a one-off card price survivable.

Two rules of thumb: a product that only earns you money once is a job, not a business — aim for repeat revenue. And never price a batch without the packaging and the courier, because those are the costs people forget.
